THE STORY

A shipwreck that isn't really a shipwreck. A rescue that isn't really a rescue.

Episode 01 — Washed Ashore

She waves through the fence like she's already expecting you.

He surfaces on wet sand with no memory of his own name and a headache that won't quit. There's a mailbox nearby with nobody's mail in it. There's a guesthouse past the tree line, and a family running it who take him in without asking a single hard question. That last part is the part he should have clocked as strange.

The rooms are clean. Dinner is generous. And somewhere behind his eyes, a version of events keeps trying to surface that doesn't line up with any of it.

PIGPEN is written by Carnby Kim — also behind the horror hit Sweet Home — with art by Beom Sick Cheon. It ran on Naver Webtoon from 2019 to 2020 and wrapped at 68 episodes, so nobody has to wait for the next drop anymore.

Three Movements

The plot doesn't build in a straight line so much as tighten, like a knot pulled from both ends. Here's roughly where the seams are.

01

Arrival

Sand, salt, a blank head. The family finds him before he manages to find himself, and the hospitality kicks in almost too fast to feel earned. He goes along with it because what else is there to do.

Episodes 1–20ish
02

The Household

Small things stop adding up. A door locked that shouldn't be. A story that changes depending on who's telling it. A boy in a pig mask nobody will explain. He starts sleeping with something sharp nearby.

Episodes 20–45ish
03

The Unraveling

The island stops behaving like an island. Every secret he thought belonged to the family turns out to be a question about himself he's been dodging since the first page.
